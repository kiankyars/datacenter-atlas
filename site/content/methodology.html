Coverage and dates

The atlas contains {{counts.physical_sites}} physical sites and {{counts.projects}} construction projects in {{counts.countries}} countries. A site is a campus or facility; a project is a building, phase or expansion at that site. Several projects can share one site.

The data was updated on {{dataset.checkpoint_date}}. Its construction observations span {{dataset.window_start}} to {{dataset.window_end}}. Each project keeps its last reported status and observation date; an Atlas update does not mean every project was rechecked. A project may since have progressed, opened, paused or been cancelled. Retrieval and review dates do not change the observation date.

Coverage follows available public sources and varies across countries and languages. The atlas is not a complete list of construction worldwide.

Selection criteria

  1. Dated physical construction. A company disclosure, government record or regulatory filing reports physical work on the named project. The observation date is recorded separately from the date the Atlas was updated. Announcements, land purchases, permits and planned opening dates alone do not qualify. Later completion or cancellation reports are checked during review.
  2. A distinct site. Each project is linked to one campus or facility and checked against existing records. Additional buildings, phases and alternative names at the same campus do not increase the site count.
  3. A sourced location. An official boundary, address, published coordinate or named map feature locates the project or its campus. Its scope and precision are recorded. Town centres, corporate offices and unrelated neighbouring buildings do not qualify.
  4. Traceable facts. Each status, location and capacity or other recorded detail links to its supporting source. Missing information remains unknown.

The data specification documents the underlying fields and review process.

Construction status

Every project records a status, the date it was observed (status_as_of) and its source. The date, rather than the page's update date, tells you how recent the construction information is.

The map groups stages by colour: blue for general construction, vermillion for early works, and green for building and fit-out. A site's colour follows its most recently observed project. The exact status is also shown in text.

Location precision

{{counts.polygon_sites}} sites have a polygon or multipolygon; the rest have a point. {{counts.official_boundary_projects}} projects use an official site boundary. A map shape may represent a parcel or campus rather than the building under construction. Each record says what its location represents:

Official boundary
An official parcel, cadastral or surveyed boundary of the site or campus.
Campus location
A point or area locating the parent campus, which may contain several projects.
Project location
A point or area locating the named project at the precision of its source.
Site reference point
A reviewed address, parcel or shared-campus reference point.
Host estate
The surrounding industrial estate or business park. Where a correction identifies this broader scope, it is labelled as an exception: the marker does not identify the project's position.

The API and downloads preserve the location's source, method, scope and any stated uncertainty. An unknown accuracy is left unknown. For a polygon, latitude and longitude are a convenient display anchor; use the full geometry for spatial analysis.

Sources

Each source record includes a URL, publisher, title, publication date where known, retrieval time, licence and SHA-256 hash of the captured content. The atlas publishes extracted facts and links, rather than redistributing source documents or imagery. See source attribution for applicable terms.

Site pages also link to the Internet Archive's nearest capture. A capture may be unavailable or differ from the material reviewed; the content hash identifies the reviewed material.

Capacity and other details

{{counts.power_observation_projects}} of {{counts.projects}} projects have a recorded power observation. Each value keeps its source, date, scope, unit and stage, such as planned or installed. These metrics measure different things:

  • Critical IT load: power available to IT equipment.
  • Gross facility power: total facility power, including cooling.
  • Grid connection: utility connection capacity.
  • Generation nameplate: on-site generation capacity.

These values should not be added together. Annual energy and PUE are recorded separately. A missing value means unknown, not zero.

Operator, owner, tenant and customer relationships, workloads and operating models require evidence for the named project and scope. Intended workloads are distinguished from operational workloads.

Imagery

Construction statuses in the current data come from source reports. No project is marked as independently verified by imagery. Where an imagery review is available, its result is recorded separately from the reported status.

Additions and corrections

Suggest a project with the new-site form, or use the correction form to update a record. Include a dated primary source and a location source. Each site page has a correction link with its identifier filled in.

Accepted changes appear in the next data update. Earlier data files remain unchanged in the data archive, so previous analyses can be reproduced. downloads/latest/ always contains the current data.

Identifiers and stable URLs

Use site_id, project_id and evidence_id to join records. Site and project identifiers are derived from stable curation keys and retained when a record is renamed or updated. Evidence identifiers refer to a particular captured source; changed content receives a new identifier.

Identifiers are never reused for a different entity. A merge or split retires identifiers that no longer describe the same entity. The archive index lists added and removed sites. Retired records remain in the archived downloads that contained them.

Site pages and api/v1/ describe the current data. A record absent from the current data returns HTTP 404 there. Archived download paths remain available.

Reproducing the data

Each archived dataset includes a manifest.json with file sizes and SHA-256 hashes; manifest.sha256 identifies the manifest. Generated formats such as SQLite and Excel have checksums in SHA256SUMS.
