What the dataset contains

The unit of analysis is the physical site: one campus or facility location, with one or more construction projects on it (a building, phase or expansion). The current version contains {{counts.physical_sites}} sites with {{counts.projects}} projects in {{counts.countries}} countries ({{counts.non_us_sites}} sites outside the United States), backed by {{counts.evidence}} evidence rows.

Projects are grouped into sites by reviewed project-to-campus identity links, compared against every site already in the atlas. This is not a global deduplication of every source that mentions a campus.

Two dates frame every version. The lifecycle reference date, {{dataset.lifecycle_reference_date}}, is fixed: each status was observed in the {{dataset.status_window_days}} days ending on it, from {{dataset.window_start}}. The checkpoint date, {{dataset.checkpoint_date}}, records when this version was reviewed and frozen. Later research, retrievals and reviews never move a status forward in time.

Selection rules

A project is included only when every rule below holds. The full contract is in docs/verified_construction_core.md and, for the v0.18 expansion, docs/expansion_200.md.

  1. Recent, authoritative physical work. A company disclosure, government record, regulatory filing or comparable authoritative source reports that physical construction on the named project had started or was progressing, as of a date between {{dataset.window_start}} and {{dataset.lifecycle_reference_date}} inclusive. A later web retrieval, page edit, permit, budget or expected opening date does not refresh that date, and completion or cancellation successors are checked before a project is accepted.
  2. A physical construction stage. The status must be a physical stage: site preparation, civil works, foundations, shell, MEP/electrical, general construction (under_construction), commissioning, or construction of an expansion. Announcements, land purchases, permitting, permits, grid-connection requests and planned dates do not qualify on their own, and neither does an operational facility.
  3. One distinct physical site. Each project is linked to exactly one reviewed parent campus. A further building, phase or alias on a campus that is already represented does not add a site.
  4. A reviewed locator. The exact named project is bound to an official boundary or to a source-specific locator, such as an official parcel, an official address point, a coordinate published by the developer or a public authority, or a named official or community map feature. The source's precision is preserved, and an unknown accuracy stays unknown. Locality centres, corporate offices, industrial-zone centroids and unrelated neighbouring buildings are rejected.
  5. Closed evidence. The status, the locator and every typed value cite evidence rows in the same dataset.
  6. Nothing unsupported. Roles, workloads, operating models, capacity, energy and efficiency are recorded only where a source states them for the right scope. Unknowns stay unknown: nothing is filled in to make a record look complete.

The release gates add cohort-level requirements on top of these rules: a site-count target and geographic diversity (minimum numbers of countries and of sites outside the United States, and a maximum share for any one country).

Physical status

Each project records its last observed physical status (last_observed_physical_status), the date it applies to (status_as_of), how it was established (status_method) and its evidence row (status_evidence_id). It is the latest qualifying observation on or before {{dataset.lifecycle_reference_date}}, not a statement about today: the project may since have been completed, paused or cancelled. A site lists the distinct statuses of its projects. Status codes and their meaning:

{{{status_legend}}}

Two status methods occur in the current data: authoritative_physical_status_update, where the source reports the state of physical work, and authoritative_construction_start, where it reports that physical construction began. The number of days between the observation and the reference date is in status_age_days_at_review.

On the maps, colour shows one of three stage groups, chosen so that they stay distinguishable for colour-blind readers: blue for general construction (under_construction), vermillion for early works (site preparation, clearing, civil works, foundations) and bluish green for building and fit-out (shell, MEP/electrical, commissioning). Any other status is grey. The exact status is always written out in text. A site with several projects takes the colour of its most recently observed project; when dates tie, a specific stage is preferred over general construction.

Locators are not footprints

Each project has one reviewed geometry, and each site carries the geometry of its projects. {{counts.polygon_sites}} of the {{counts.physical_sites}} sites have a polygon or multipolygon; the rest are points. Only {{counts.official_boundary_projects}} projects use an official site boundary. Everything else is a locator: it shows where the project or its campus is, at a stated scope and precision. It is not the outline of a building or of the construction works.

official_boundary
An official parcel, cadastral or surveyed boundary of the project site or its parent campus. Even this is not a building footprint or the extent of current works.
campus_locator
Locates the parent campus or host development (for example a campus or address point, or a parcel or estate polygon), not necessarily the current project.
project_locator
Locates the named project itself, at the precision of its source.
reviewed_site_locator
A reviewed reference point labelled by what it actually marks, such as a shared-campus reference point, a facility address point or a parcel reference point.

Further fields qualify every geometry. geometry_authority_class says who published it (an official or first-party source, a community source, or a community-mapped feature such as OpenStreetMap). geometry_scope_class and geometry_source_entity_kind say what the source geometry represents. geometry_method and geometry_derivation say how it was obtained, and geometry_precision_scope states its limits in words. horizontal_uncertainty_metres gives a numeric uncertainty where the source supports one; otherwise horizontal_uncertainty_unknown_reason explains why there is none.

The latitude and longitude columns are a display anchor for maps and lists. For a point they repeat the locator, with the same scope and precision limits. For a polygon or multipolygon the full geometry is the published locator, and the anchor is only a convenient marker position.

Evidence and provenance

Every evidence row in evidence.csv records the source URL, publisher, kind and title; the roles the row plays (physical status, geometry, identity context, a typed metric and so on); the publication date where known; the retrieval time; a SHA-256 hash of the captured source content; and the source's licence and attribution. The atlas stores compact factual extractions with links; it does not redistribute source documents, imagery or media. Each version's manifest.json binds every file to its size and SHA-256 digest. Source terms are summarised on the attribution page.

Capacity, roles and workloads stay unknown unless evidenced

Capacity is unknown unless a typed observation exists. {{counts.power_observation_projects}} of the {{counts.projects}} projects carry a typed power observation. Each observation keeps its metric, stage, low, base and high values, unit, method, confidence, as-of date and evidence. The metrics measure different things and must not be added together or substituted for one another:

  • Critical IT load (critical_it_mw): power available to IT equipment.
  • Gross facility power (gross_facility_mw): total facility power, including cooling and other loads.
  • Grid connection (grid_connection_mw): utility connection capacity, which is not the data-centre load.
  • Generation nameplate (generation_nameplate_mw): on-site generation capacity, which is not load either.

Stages such as planned, design and contracted describe intent or agreements, not installed or measured capacity. Annual energy and PUE are kept separate from power and are not estimated without scoped inputs. A missing value is unknown, never zero, and absent typed values carry an explicit *_unknown_reason.

Roles (operator, owner, tenant, customer, user), workloads and operating models are recorded only with specific evidence; roles also carry a relationship scope, and workloads a deployment scope. A workload observation records a source's classification and whether it is intended or operational; it is not proof of what a facility runs. Company names in site and project names identify facilities and do not by themselves establish any role.

Imagery and verification

In the current dataset no project is marked as independently verified by imagery (independent_imagery_verification). A small number of projects carry a satellite or drone imagery-review outcome, kept for provenance; none of these outcomes changes a status or creates a construction or capacity claim. The verification_posture field states what each record rests on: a recent authoritative physical observation plus either a reviewed site locator or an official boundary.

Release gates

A version can be published as final only when every gate passes. The current version has release status {{dataset.release_status}} and is not publishable as final. The table is generated from the dataset's selection-report.json.

{{{gates_table}}}

Five gates measure scale and diversity: the site count, the sites added beyond the frozen {{last_preview.label}} baseline, the number of countries, the number of sites outside the United States and the largest single-country share. The others concern verification and release: an imagery-review outcome for every project, a blind re-review of a fixed sample drawn from the whole cohort, a byte-exact rebuild from a fresh clone of the public repository, and an explicit publication decision. The draft builder never authorizes publication, so reaching the site-count target alone does not make a version final.

Versions and corrections

There are {{versions.count}} dataset versions: the v0.1 to v0.17 previews (the last of which, the {{last_preview.label}}, has {{last_preview.counts.physical_sites}} sites) and the reviewed v0.18 draft checkpoints that extend it. Each version is a complete, frozen snapshot, so never add counts from different versions together. Older versions stay byte-for-byte unchanged so that any earlier result can be audited, and each has pinned downloads that never change; downloads/latest/ follows the current version. Errors found later are fixed by a documented, versioned correction in a new checkpoint, not by rewriting a published version. See all versions.

Limitations

  • Not a census. The atlas is a reviewed sample held to a strict evidence bar. Coverage follows what sources publish and is uneven across countries and languages; many projects never disclose a dated physical status and a precise location.
  • Last observed physical status, not current status. Statuses are fixed at the lifecycle reference date and age from then on.
  • Locators are not footprints. Most sites are points. A polygon may outline a parcel, a campus, a host estate or an existing building used only as a locator; even a building outline is not necessarily the building under construction.
  • Sparse typed values. Capacity, roles, workloads and operating models are unknown for most projects.
  • Validation is not ground truth. Hash checks show that the data matches its pinned sources and review decisions. They do not independently establish physical truth or resolve undisclosed campus aliases.
  • No benchmark parity. No licensed row-level benchmark is available, so no completeness or accuracy parity with commercial trackers, such as SemiAnalysis, is claimed.
  • Not advice. The data is provided as is and is not legal, surveying, investment, engineering or operational advice.

Reproducibility

Every file of every version is bound by size and SHA-256 in its manifest.json, and the manifest's own digest is in manifest.sha256. Each v0.18 checkpoint can be re-derived and compared byte for byte from its hash-pinned inputs with the repository's draft builder:

python3 scripts/build_expansion_200_draft.py --batch <checkpoint> --validate-only

This website, its API and its downloads are generated from the tracked dataset by a standard-library Python builder; the same inputs produce byte-identical output. Build and test instructions are in the repository README; the data itself is on the data page and through the API.
